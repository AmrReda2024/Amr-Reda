What has changed in Egyptian law that your existing Egypt memo does not cover

61days to 31 October 2026

The Personal Data Protection Law becomes enforceable

Law 151/2020 has been on the books since 2020, but its Executive Regulations were only issued by Decree 816 of 1 November 2025. The twelve-month grace period they opened ends on 31 October 2026. After that date, licensing through the Personal Data Protection Centre is a condition of processing, not a formality to schedule later — and the law reaches controllers outside Egypt who process the data of people in it.

  • 1 June 2024 Merger control became suspensory. Under Law 3/2005 as amended by Law 175/2022, qualifying transactions must be cleared by the Egyptian Competition Authority before closing. Deals structured on the old post-closing notification regime are being signed with the wrong conditions precedent.
  • 1 September 2025 Labour Law 14/2025 replaced Law 12/2003 in full. Templates, handbooks and secondment arrangements drafted against the 2003 law are now drafted against a repealed statute.
  • 1 October 2025 Specialised labour courts opened. Employment disputes no longer follow the general civil track, which changes both timetable and forum strategy.
  • 1 November 2025 PDPL Executive Regulations issued by Decree 816. Cross-border transfer, electronic marketing and surveillance each require their own licence, separately applied for.

The entry sequence

Six gates. Each has an authority, an instrument, and a way entrants fail it.

Order matters more than speed. Gates 3 and 5 are the two that most often force a signed transaction to be reopened, because both bind before completion rather than after it. Open any gate for the failure mode.

How entrants fail it
The form is chosen for speed of registration and only afterwards tested against the sector licence in gate 2 and the merger analysis in gate 3. Where the licence the business actually needs cannot be held by the vehicle already registered, the entrant restructures at its own cost — after the group has announced the entry.
How entrants fail it
Commercial timetables are built on the registration date at gate 1 and treat the sector authorisation as an administrative follow-up. Distribution agreements, supply commitments and launch dates are then signed against a licence that does not yet exist, and the counterparty holds the entrant to dates it cannot lawfully meet.
Route
FRA-regulated activities follow a separate route.
Thresholds
Domestic test: combined Egyptian turnover or assets above EGP 900 million, with at least two parties each above EGP 200 million. Global test: combined worldwide turnover or assets above EGP 7.5 billion.
How entrants fail it
The global test is read as an Egypt-nexus test. A transaction with modest Egyptian revenue but large worldwide figures is assumed to be out of scope, the filing is not made, and the parties close. The defect is discovered on the next transaction or in diligence by the buyer after them.
Counterparts
Contracts are made in four counterparts.
How entrants fail it
Group templates are localised once, at entry, and then reused. Contracts issued after 1 September 2025 on a 12/2003 basis carry terms drawn from a repealed statute, and the first dispute is heard by a court built specifically to hear it.
Reach
Extraterritorial. A controller with no Egyptian entity can still be within scope.
How entrants fail it
The group treats its existing GDPR programme as sufficient and schedules Egypt for the next compliance cycle. The Egyptian requirement is licensing, not documentation, and the licence is granted to an applicant — a mapped data flow with no licence behind it is still unlawful on 1 November 2026.
How entrants fail it
One registration is treated as covering the whole programme. In practice the routine operations of a foreign group — sending HR and customer data to a parent, running campaigns from a regional marketing platform, operating cameras on site — each sit under their own licence, and the ones nobody applied for are the ones that surface in an inspection.
